01 / THE CHALLENGE

Water goes down the drain.
Can its value stay with us?

Washbasins and showers can generate greywater that may be suitable for certain non-drinking purposes after an appropriate treatment process. When this water is simply discharged, an opportunity for reuse may be lost.

🚿 01

Everyday water use

Hostels, colleges and residential buildings use water for bathing, handwashing and daily routines.

🌀 02

Lost reuse potential

Some lightly used water may be recoverable for suitable non-potable applications with proper treatment.

🌿 03

A local opportunity

A carefully designed local system could help reduce demand for fresh water in selected campus activities.

02 / INTRODUCING REWAVE

Meet the next wave
of responsible water use.

REWAVE is a proposed local greywater collection and reuse system. It aims to collect water from selected washbasins and showers, treat it through an engineered filtration process, check its quality and direct approved water towards selected non-potable uses.

💧

What is REWAVE?

A concept for giving suitable wastewater another purpose through collection, treatment, quality verification, storage and controlled reuse.

03 / WHY REWAVE?

Not just recycling water.
Reimagining it locally.

REWAVE's proposed distinction is its focus on a local, manageable, monitored reuse loop designed around the everyday needs of a campus or neighbourhood.

📍

1. Local-first approach

Instead of starting with a city-wide system, REWAVE explores treatment near the place where suitable greywater is generated and reused.

🧩

2. Modular design

The concept can be evaluated in stages: begin with one selected building, study the results, then consider adding capacity if justified.

🚦

3. A visible monitoring concept

Red, yellow and green indicators can communicate system status. Actual release decisions must depend on validated instruments, testing and safety controls.

🌱

4. Multiple reuse pathways

Subject to water quality and local approval, treated water could be considered for landscaping, suitable cleaning tasks or toilet flushing.

💰

5. Budget-conscious planning

An editable budget helps the team compare components, quantities and costs against a proposed ₹30 lakh planning cap.

📈

6. Measure first, scale later

Flow measurements, water-quality results, maintenance records and actual reuse demand can guide whether expansion makes sense.

What makes the concept worth exploring?

REWAVE connects a local water challenge with a practical design question: how can a community recover suitable water safely, understand its performance and make informed decisions about reuse?

These are proposed design features, not claims that REWAVE is already cheaper, more efficient or more sustainable than existing systems.

PROPOSED WATER JOURNEY

A controlled reuse loop

Collection alone does not make water safe. The system must be designed for the contaminants present, tested for its intended use and operated with a safe route for water that fails quality requirements.

  • Dedicated collection and conveyance.
  • Screening and appropriate treatment stages.
  • Quality verification before release.
  • Separate storage and distribution for approved uses.
  • Safe diversion of off-specification water.

10 / WHERE COULD IT BE USED?

One source.
Several possible uses.

Each reuse option must be assessed individually. Water appropriate for one use is not automatically appropriate for another.

🌿

Landscaping

Selected lawns and plants, where the water quality and irrigation method are suitable.

🚽

Toilet flushing

Non-potable flushing supply using a dedicated, protected plumbing network.

🧹

Floor cleaning

Suitable non-clinical cleaning applications where quality and hygiene requirements permit.

🏢

Selected utilities

Other approved building uses after specific technical and regulatory assessment.

Important safety boundary

Reclaimed water is not drinking water. Hospital use, even in non-clinical areas, needs a separate risk assessment and explicit approval. Do not use the water for drinking, cooking, bathing or handwashing unless a separately validated treatment system and applicable standards explicitly permit it.

13 / FROM IDEA TO PILOT

Start small.
Prove it. Then grow.

A safe project starts with measurements and validation, not equipment installation alone.

01

Survey the campus

Identify candidate sources, daily flow, existing drainage and potential non-potable demand.

02

Test the water

Characterise the greywater and determine the treatment and quality requirements for each intended use.

03

Design the treatment system

Have qualified professionals select processes, pipework, storage, sensors and safety controls.

04

Validate and commission

Test performance, confirm regulatory compliance, train operators and establish a fail-safe operating plan.

05

Monitor and improve

Record water recovered, water reused, test results, downtime, maintenance and actual costs.

🔬

What should success look like?

  • Water quality consistently meets requirements for its intended use.
  • Reuse is separated from drinking-water plumbing.
  • Unsafe or off-specification water is automatically held or diverted where appropriate.
  • Actual water recovered and reused is measured.
  • Maintenance, energy use and operating costs are documented.
  • Expansion is based on evidence from the pilot.
THE REWAVE PRINCIPLE

Reuse is only a success when it is safe, useful, measurable and appropriate for the community that operates it.
